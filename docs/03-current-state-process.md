# Current-State Process Analysis

## Purpose
This document outlines the current-state process environment for the Retail Operational Integrity & Exception Management case study. Its purpose is to show how operational issues are currently identified, communicated, handled, and resolved across functions, and where the main process, control, and visibility weaknesses exist.

## Current-State Overview
In the current state, operational changes and exception-related issues are handled across multiple teams, systems, and communication channels. Although core retail systems are already in place, the business does not operate with a single, consistently governed process for managing operational data updates and exception resolution.

As a result, issue handling depends heavily on manual coordination, fragmented visibility, and inconsistent follow-through.

## Evidence-Based Current-State Context
The current-state environment reflects a common operational pattern in Canadian SMB retail: core systems exist, but process execution remains under-integrated. This creates repeated conditions where operational updates, issue handling, and cross-functional coordination rely on manual effort rather than structured workflow control.

## Current-State Process Flow

### 1. Operational issue or required update is identified
A store-level or operational issue is identified through daily execution. This may involve pricing, inventory, product information, or another operational exception.

### 2. Business user records or communicates the issue
The issue is captured through available channels such as spreadsheet tracking, email, message-based communication, or informal escalation.

### 3. Request is handed off to another function
The issue is passed to the individual or team responsible for system-level changes, operational updates, or follow-up action.

### 4. Request is reviewed and interpreted manually
The receiving team reviews the request and determines what action is needed, how urgent it is, and whether the information provided is complete enough to act on.

### 5. Update or correction is applied
The required update, correction, or follow-up action is completed within the relevant system or workflow, depending on ownership and system access.

### 6. Validation and follow-through occur inconsistently
In some cases, the issue is confirmed as resolved. In other cases, validation is delayed, incomplete, or assumed rather than formally verified.

### 7. Exceptions remain open, recur, or reappear
Because visibility, ownership, and process control are weak, some issues are not fully resolved and may return later as repeated operational exceptions.

## Current-State Characteristics
The current process environment is characterized by:
- fragmented operational visibility
- manual handoffs between business and system owners
- inconsistent ownership and prioritization
- weak traceability of issue status
- delayed validation and follow-through
- recurring operational exceptions caused by process and control gaps

## Quantified Current-State Pain
At the current-state level, the business problem can be expressed through measurable operational impact areas such as:
- number of unresolved exceptions at a given point in time
- average exception resolution time
- number of repeated exceptions within a reporting period
- percentage of issues requiring manual follow-up
- percentage of issues resolved without formal validation
- number of cross-functional handoffs required per issue

These measures are included to frame the problem in operational terms and support later KPI definition.

## Key Current-State Gaps

### Gap 1: No single operational view
Issue-related information is spread across people, systems, and communication channels, making it difficult to maintain a trusted view of what requires attention.

### Gap 2: Manual coordination drives execution
A significant portion of issue handling depends on manual communication and handoff rather than structured workflow control.

### Gap 3: Prioritization is inconsistent
Urgency and business impact are not always evaluated through a shared framework, which creates uneven response handling.

### Gap 4: Ownership is not always clear
Responsibility for action, follow-up, and validation may be distributed across multiple roles without clear accountability.

### Gap 5: Resolution tracking is weak
The business does not consistently maintain structured visibility into issue status, aging, or recurrence.

## Baseline KPI Candidates
The current-state process suggests the following baseline KPI areas for later solution evaluation:
- exception backlog volume
- exception resolution time
- repeated exception frequency
- ownership visibility
- validation completion rate
- manual follow-up dependency

## Analysis Summary
The current-state process is not failing because the business lacks systems altogether. The core problem is that operational integrity is weakened by fragmented data handling, under-integrated workflows, manual request routing, and limited exception governance.

This creates a process environment in which issues are often managed reactively rather than through structured visibility, prioritization, ownership, control, and measurable follow-through.

# Stakeholder Analysis

## Purpose
This document identifies the key stakeholders involved in the Retail Operational Integrity & Exception Management case study and assesses their role, business interest, level of influence, impact exposure, and importance to solution design and delivery.

## Stakeholder Groups

### 1. Store Operations Manager
**Role:** Owns day-to-day store execution and operational follow-through at location level.  
**Business Interest:** Faster issue resolution, better visibility into operational exceptions, and fewer manual escalations.  
**Level of Influence:** High  
**Level of Impact:** High  
**Key Concern:** Store issues are often identified late, managed through manual follow-up, and resolved inconsistently across teams.  
**Engagement Approach:** Primary stakeholder for discovery, current-state pain points, workflow validation, and future-state review.  
**Decision Relevance:** Provides operational input into exception prioritization, escalation needs, and process practicality.  
**Risk if Misaligned:** The proposed solution may look good on paper but fail in real day-to-day store operations.

### 2. Inventory and Fulfillment Coordinator
**Role:** Supports stock accuracy, order handling, replenishment follow-through, and exception resolution across inventory-related activities.  
**Business Interest:** Better control over discrepancies, clearer ownership of exceptions, and more reliable issue tracking.  
**Level of Influence:** Medium  
**Level of Impact:** High  
**Key Concern:** Critical exceptions are spread across systems, updates are inconsistent, and follow-up is not always visible or traceable.  
**Engagement Approach:** Key stakeholder for identifying recurring exception categories, current-state gaps, and control breakdowns.  
**Decision Relevance:** Informs inventory-related business rules, issue routing, and operational control needs.  
**Risk if Misaligned:** Important exception flows may be oversimplified or disconnected from actual execution realities.

### 3. E-commerce / Systems Support Lead
**Role:** Controls or manages updates within retail systems, platform settings, and operational data changes that require system-level execution.  
**Business Interest:** Cleaner requests, stronger control, fewer manual corrections, and more consistent update handling.  
**Level of Influence:** High  
**Level of Impact:** Medium to High  
**Key Concern:** Business-side changes often arrive through incomplete, inconsistent, or manually prioritized requests, creating rework and control issues.  
**Engagement Approach:** Critical stakeholder for understanding current system constraints, update ownership, and control points.  
**Decision Relevance:** Provides input into feasibility, permissions, update workflow structure, and control requirements.  
**Risk if Misaligned:** The future-state design may ignore system realities, ownership boundaries, or platform control limitations.

### 4. Retail Owner / General Manager
**Role:** Accountable for business performance, operational effectiveness, and management visibility across functions.  
**Business Interest:** Better control, reduced operational friction, improved issue resolution, and stronger cross-functional execution.  
**Level of Influence:** Very High  
**Level of Impact:** High  
**Key Concern:** Repeated operational issues reduce efficiency, consume management attention, and negatively affect customer experience and business reliability.  
**Engagement Approach:** Executive stakeholder for business priorities, expected value, and final solution alignment.  
**Decision Relevance:** Confirms scope priorities, business objectives, and desired management outcomes.  
**Risk if Misaligned:** The case study may solve operational pain without clearly linking it to business value.

## Secondary Stakeholders

### 5. Store Associates
**Role:** Frontline staff interacting with customers and executing store-level activities affected by pricing, stock, and operational updates.  
**Business Interest:** Accurate information, smoother execution, and fewer customer-facing disruptions.  
**Level of Influence:** Low to Medium  
**Level of Impact:** Medium  
**Key Concern:** Operational inconsistencies often surface during customer interaction, creating confusion and avoidable friction.  
**Engagement Approach:** Useful source of frontline pain-point validation and customer-impact examples.  
**Decision Relevance:** Helps validate where execution problems become visible in practice.  
**Risk if Misaligned:** The analysis may miss how issues actually appear at point of execution.

### 6. Customers
**Role:** End users affected by the quality and consistency of retail execution across channels.  
**Business Interest:** Accurate product, pricing, inventory, and fulfillment information.  
**Level of Influence:** Indirect  
**Level of Impact:** High  
**Key Concern:** Operational inconsistency creates poor service experience, reduced trust, and avoidable friction.  
**Engagement Approach:** Represented indirectly through operational pain points, service failures, and business impact analysis.  
**Decision Relevance:** Helps connect internal process issues to customer-facing outcomes.  
**Risk if Misaligned:** The solution may improve internal control without improving customer experience.

## Stakeholder Assessment Summary

The problem is cross-functional in nature. Business users experience the operational pain directly, while system control, update ownership, data handling, and exception routing are distributed across multiple roles. This makes stakeholder alignment essential to accurate current-state analysis, realistic future-state design, and effective solution framing.

From a Business Analysis perspective, the most critical stakeholders for this case study are:
- Store Operations Manager
- Inventory and Fulfillment Coordinator
- E-commerce / Systems Support Lead
- Retail Owner / General Manager

These stakeholders collectively represent operational execution, exception handling, systems control, and business accountability.
